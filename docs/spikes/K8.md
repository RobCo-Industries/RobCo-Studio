# Spike K8: the whole window through an animated CRT shader

## The question

Can Studio pass its whole window through an animated CRT shader in eframe 0.37 on wgpu, at a cost a person accepts on a laptop? The effects asked for are phosphor bloom, vignette, scanlines, screen curvature, phosphor persistence, signal noise, flickering, an RGB shadow mask and chromatic aberration. A slow rolling band was asked for too.

Short answer: yes, inside eframe, with no fork. The passes cost about 0.4 ms of GPU time per frame at 1280 by 720 and about 1 ms at 2560 by 1440 on one Mac, an M1 Max laptop. In one run, animating at 60 frames a second cost 13 % of one core, against 0 % for today's idle window. Code text stays readable at the tasteful setting. The shadow mask and the aberration hurt text and add nothing a one-colour tube would show.

## Method

### Where egui's picture goes, from the source

These are the 0.37.0 sources in the cargo registry.

- `eframe/src/native/wgpu_integration.rs:833` calls `Painter::paint_and_update_textures` once a frame. Line 874 calls `post_rendering`, after the frame is presented. There is no step in between that the app can reach.
- `egui-wgpu/src/winit.rs:490` is `paint_and_update_textures`. It takes the surface texture at line 598, opens the `egui_render` pass on it at line 684, draws with `Renderer::render` at line 735 and presents at line 792. egui draws straight into the window's surface. No texture with egui's picture exists for a later pass to read.

The four candidates:

| Candidate | Result |
|---|---|
| A paint callback inside egui's own render pass | Not alone: the callback draws into the surface pass and cannot read that surface. But its `prepare` step, `egui-wgpu/src/renderer.rs:89`, called at line 1107, gets the device, the queue and a command encoder before egui's pass begins. That is the hook, see below. |
| A hook that eframe gives before presenting | None exists. `post_rendering` runs after `present`. The screenshot path, `ViewportCommand::Screenshot`, renders to a capture texture and copies it back to the CPU: a frame late and far too slow for every frame. |
| A second viewport | Ruled out. Each viewport has its own surface and its own pass. It cannot read another viewport's picture. |
| Our own `winit` and `egui-wgpu` loop without eframe | Works, but it is not needed. It would cost Studio the glue listed below. |

The hook used, inside eframe, in `redirect_to_crt` in `spikes/k8/src/lib.rs`:

1. At the end of `App::ui`, take every shape egui painted this frame with `Context::graphics_mut` (`egui/src/context.rs:1300`) and `GraphicLayers::drain` (`egui/src/layers.rs:236`).
2. Tessellate them with `Context::tessellate` (`context.rs:3358`).
3. Paint one paint callback over the whole window, carrying those triangles and the font atlas.
4. In the callback's `prepare`, render the triangles with our own `egui_wgpu::Renderer` into an offscreen texture, then run the CRT passes. In its `paint`, draw the result into eframe's pass with one triangle.

Input, AccessKit, the clipboard, IME, the menu bar and scale stay eframe's: the widgets are still built in eframe's own context. Only where the pixels go changes.

Three costs of this hook:

- `Areas::order` is `pub(crate)` (`egui/src/memory/mod.rs:1246`), so `drain` gets an empty order. Layers keep their order class, background, middle, foreground, tooltip, but two overlapping windows of the same class may draw in the wrong order. A one-line upstream change, making `order` public, would remove this.
- Our renderer needs the font atlas. eframe hands texture changes only to its own renderer, so the spike sends the whole atlas each frame. Studio would send it only when it changes, and would also need its own images, the wordmark and the icons, sent to our renderer.
- eframe's renderer still uploads the same atlas. That is wasted but small.

What leaving eframe would cost, for the record. `crates/robco-studio` uses from eframe: `run_native` and `NativeOptions` with the title, the inner and minimum size; the `accesskit` and `clipboard` features; the `wayland` and `x11` features; `CreationContext`. `egui-winit` alone still gives AccessKit, the clipboard, IME and the scale factor. Studio would write the event loop, the surface and its resize and loss handling, the minimum size and title through `winit`, the app icon through `winit` on Windows and Linux plus eframe's macOS dock icon code, the repaint scheduling, multi-monitor scale changes, and the wake-up that `muda`'s menu events use to reach the window. That is several hundred lines that eframe keeps tested.

### The passes

`spikes/k8/src/crt.wgsl`, five passes, one full-screen triangle each:

1. egui's picture into a full-size texture.
2. Bright pixels into a half-size texture.
3. and 4. A 9-tap blur, across and down.
5. The composite: curvature, aberration, bloom, scanlines that drift down 6 points a second with a 3-point period, the rolling band every 6 s, the shadow mask, noise, flicker of up to 3 %, vignette, and persistence from the previous output.

Each effect has a strength from 0 to 1. Zero turns it off. With every strength at zero the output matches egui's picture.

### Curvature and the pointer

The curve maps a place on the glass to the place in egui's picture that is drawn there. `warp` in `lib.rs` is the same formula as `curve` in the shader. In `App::raw_input_hook`, `map_input` passes every pointer and touch position through `warp` before egui sees it. So a pointer over a drawn button is sent to egui at the button.

Proof, two ways:

- `tests/curve.rs`, no GPU: find where a point on the button's bottom edge is drawn, click there through the mapping, and the button reports a click. At strong curvature the same click without the mapping misses. This needed egui's `interact_radius` set to zero, because egui grows each hit area by 5 points, which hides small misses.
- `render`, on a GPU: at full curvature, three points of the button, top, left and bottom edges, are found in the rendered picture where `drawn_at` says. Each pixel there is the button's fill.

What curvature does to the rest:

- Hover, text selection and scroll areas all read the mapped pointer, so they agree with what is drawn. The scroll wheel carries no position.
- AccessKit rectangles stay in egui's flat picture. A screen reader's highlight box or a magnifier that follows focus would be off by up to the curve's move: 4 points near this button at full strength, more at the corners. At the tasteful strength it is under 1 point.
- IME candidate windows are placed from egui's flat rectangle, so they drift the same way.

### Measuring

- `cargo run --release --bin render -- <dir>` renders offscreen: the pictures, the curvature check, and the timings. Timing is wall time per frame with the GPU waited for each frame, mean of 120 frames, after 10 warm-up frames. "egui only" renders egui into the texture with no passes.
- `cargo run --release` opens a 640 by 400 window. `K8_MODE=idle|crt|overlay` and `K8_FPS`. It runs 11 s, reads the process CPU time from `ps -o time=` at 1 s and 11 s, prints, and closes itself. One run each.
- The CI job [`spike-k8.yml`](../../.github/workflows/spike-k8.yml) runs on the three hosted runners: format, build, clippy with `-D warnings`, the tests, and `render`. `render` prints "not run: no GPU adapter" and exits 0 when wgpu finds no adapter. Any other failure fails the job.
- `tests/wgsl.rs` parses and validates the WGSL with `naga` 30.0.2, the front end wgpu 30 uses. It needs no GPU, so it runs on every runner.

## Results

### Cost on one Mac

An Apple M1 Max laptop, Metal, release build. GPU numbers from `render`, CPU numbers from the window.

| Size, physical pixels | egui only | Passes, all effects strong | Cost of the passes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1280 by 720 | 0.46 ms | 0.85 ms | 0.39 ms |
| 2560 by 1440 | 0.59 ms | 1.56 ms | 0.97 ms |

The composite pass runs every effect each frame whatever its strength: there is no branch. So one effect alone costs the same as all of them, and the single-effect runs, 0.73 to 1.03 ms at 1280 by 720 and 0.95 to 3.16 ms at 2560 by 1440, differ by noise only. Wall time with a wait on each frame is a rough measure. GPU timestamp queries would be finer.

| Window, 640 by 400 points, Retina | Frames in 11 s | CPU, one core = 100 % |
|---|---|---|
| Idle, no animation, as today | 27 | 0 % |
| CRT, animated, 60 a second asked | 678 | 13 % |
| CRT, animated, 30 a second | 323 | 6 % |
| CRT, animated, 15 a second | 166 | 3 % |
| Plan B overlay, 60 a second | 624 | 6 % |

A first idle run showed 418 frames and 6 %. The pointer was over the window then. The second run, with nothing touching the window, is the one in the table.

To animate, the window must call `request_repaint_after(1/fps)` every frame. Animating only while focused works: `ctx.input(|i| i.viewport().focused)` is there each frame, and an unfocused window can stop asking. The CRT look then freezes on its last frame, which still shows the still effects.

### The pictures

The pictures are not committed, and no other spike page holds pictures. So only the notes below describe them. 1280 by 720 at one pixel per point unless said.

- `none.png`: the stand-in, flat and sharp.
- `only-*.png`, one per effect. Bloom gives every letter and button a soft green glow, the thing egui cannot do. Scanlines read as fine dark lines. Persistence leaves a short fading trail behind the moving block. Noise is a fine grain. Flicker and the band are hard to see in one still. Curvature bends the panes and blacks the corners.
- `all-on.png`: the tasteful setting. Bloom 0.35, vignette 0.5, scanlines 0.3, curvature 0.15, persistence 0.5, noise 0.15, flicker 0.3, band 0.4, mask and aberration 0. Code text is sharp and easy to read. The look is clearly a green tube.
- `all-on-2x.png`: the same at 2560 by 1440, two pixels per point, as on that Mac's screen. Text is sharper still.
- `all-strong.png`: every effect at 1. Code is hard to read: the mask, the aberration and the curve smear small letters. It is a look for a splash screen, not for work.
- `motion-0.png` to `motion-2.png`: the tasteful setting at 2.0, 2.5 and 3.0 s. The scanlines move 3 points, a full period, between frames, the band moves down, the block moves.
- `compare-mono-vs-mask.png` and `compare-mono-vs-mask-zoom.png`: left "MONO", the tasteful setting. Right "MASK", the same with the mask and the aberration at 0.6. On a green-only picture the mask has nothing in red or blue to show. It only darkens two columns in three, so the picture is darker with fine vertical stripes. The aberration puts faint red and blue fringes on green letters, which a one-colour tube never had. In the zoom, the letters on the right are striped and less crisp, and the button labels are hard to read. Text stays readable with the mask up to about 0.3 and the aberration up to about 0.2. Above that it tires the eye.
- `plan-b.png`: plan B at 60 a second. Scanlines as a faint repeating texture, the band, the flicker.

Is text sharp enough to read code for hours at the strength that looks right? Yes, at `all-on.png`, on a Retina screen. On a one-pixel-per-point screen the scanlines at 0.3 cross every letter; that is still readable but less comfortable. Nobody read code for hours through it: that is not proven.

### Plan B, no post-process

`Overlay` in `lib.rs` paints, on egui's top layer: a 1 by 3 texture repeated over the screen with its texture coordinates moving, a gradient band, and a full-screen dark rectangle whose alpha flickers. It costs 6 % CPU at 60 a second here, half the CRT path. It gives no bloom, no persistence, no curvature, no mask and no aberration: none of those can be drawn over the picture, they must read it.

Ubuntu could get a software adapter by installing Mesa's lavapipe, `mesa-vulkan-drivers`. That was not tried. Runner timings are from virtual or software GPUs and say nothing about a laptop. The Windows numbers do say one thing: on a machine that falls back to a software adapter, the passes take a fifth of a second to more than a second a frame. Studio must turn the effect off when the adapter is a CPU one, `wgpu::DeviceType::Cpu`.

## What it means for Studio

- Bloom can replace the hand-made halo on labels. It glows every letter and every bright shape the same way, with no per-widget code.
- An off switch sets every strength to zero and skips the passes: Studio then draws as today, idle at 0 %.
- A low-motion setting keeps the still effects, bloom, vignette, the mask, still scanlines, curvature, and turns off drift, the band, flicker, noise and persistence. The window then needs no repaint each frame: the passes run only when egui repaints, at no idle cost.
- The high legibility mode turns everything off, or keeps bloom low at most.
- A screen reader sees no change: the hook leaves egui's widget tree alone. Only with curvature on do AccessKit rectangles stop matching the drawn picture. Curvature should be off when AccessKit is active, or Studio should map the rectangles too.
- Flicker is a risk for people with photosensitive epilepsy or migraine. The flicker here is 3 % at most, 30 times a second. It was not checked against a published flash standard. The rolling band and noise also change brightness. Default flicker and band to off, follow the system's reduce-motion setting, and never flash more than three times a second at large contrast.

## What stays unknown

- GPU time on a weaker laptop, an integrated Intel or AMD GPU, or Linux with Mesa. Only one Mac was measured.
- A window at 2560 by 1440 was not opened: the large size was timed offscreen only.
- Whether reading code for hours through the effect is comfortable. Nobody tried.
- Persistence decays per frame, so it fades twice as fast at 60 a second as at 30. Studio would scale it by the frame time.
- Overlapping areas of the same order may draw in the wrong order with the hook, until `Areas::order` is public upstream.
- The wordmark and icon textures were not sent to our renderer: the stand-in has none.
